# PW-009: Stale Tier Zero accounts

**Category:** Hygiene
**Typical owner:** AD / identity team

## What this finding means
These accounts are enabled and hold Tier Zero privileges, but they have not logged on within the
configured period (90 days by default) or have never logged on at all. Nobody appears to use them,
yet whoever controls them can still take over the domain.

## Why attackers care
Dormant privileged accounts are ideal targets. Their passwords are often old or reused, and
nobody notices when they are used again. An attacker who guesses, sprays or cracks such a password
gets Tier Zero access through a legitimate account (Valid Accounts: Domain Accounts, MITRE
ATT&CK T1078.002). Unused accounts also tend to sit outside monitoring and MFA rollouts.

## Before you change anything
- Confirm with the account owner (from the description, the manager attribute or your IAM
  system) that the account is not needed.
- Service accounts may authenticate without updating lastLogonTimestamp the way you expect.
  Check for recent 4624 / 4768 / 4769 events for the account on domain controllers before disabling.
- lastLogonTimestamp replicates with up to a 14-day lag. Treat dates as approximate.
- For break-glass accounts that are meant to be unused, document them and accept the finding
  as a risk in Settings, with a review date, instead of disabling them.

## Fix
### Option A: Disable the account (recommended)
1. Open **Active Directory Users and Computers** (dsa.msc).
2. Find the account, right-click > **Disable Account**.
3. Add a note in the **Description** field (date, ticket number) and leave it disabled for a
   grace period (for example 30 days) before deleting it.

### Option B: Remove the privileged group memberships
If the account must stay enabled, for example for a rarely used process:
1. Open the account > **Member Of** tab.
2. Remove Domain Admins, Enterprise Admins, Administrators and any other Tier Zero group.
3. Grant only the specific rights the process needs.

### PowerShell
```powershell
Import-Module ActiveDirectory

# Review last logon and group memberships first
Get-ADUser -Identity "<Account>" -Properties LastLogonDate, MemberOf, Description |
    Select-Object SamAccountName, LastLogonDate, Description, MemberOf

# Option A: disable and annotate
Disable-ADAccount -Identity "<Account>"
Set-ADUser -Identity "<Account>" -Description "Disabled <date> - stale Tier Zero account, ticket <ID>"

# Option B: remove from a privileged group instead
Remove-ADGroupMember -Identity "Domain Admins" -Members "<Account>" -Confirm:$false
```

## Verify
```powershell
Get-ADUser -Identity "<Account>" -Properties Enabled, MemberOf |
    Select-Object SamAccountName, Enabled, MemberOf
```
Collect again (or use Retest on the finding) and confirm the finding ID is gone (or lists fewer accounts).

## Rollback
- Re-enable the account with `Enable-ADAccount -Identity "<Account>"`.
- Re-add removed group memberships with `Add-ADGroupMember`. Record the original
  memberships (the review command above) before removing them.
