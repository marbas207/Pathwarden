# PW-022: Account or group outside a Privilege Zone controls a zone object

**Category:** Attack path
**Typical owner:** Owner of the Privilege Zone (for example the server team for "Tier 1 servers"), with the AD team for permissions

## What this finding means
An account or group that is neither in the named Privilege Zone nor in Tier Zero has a right over an
object inside the zone. That could be an AD permission (GenericAll, WriteDacl, WriteOwner,
ForceChangePassword, AddMember, ...), local administrator or remote access rights on a zone
computer, delegation rights, or a GPO link. Whoever controls that account or group can take over the
zone object, so the zone boundary your tiering model assumes does not hold.

## Why attackers care
Attackers rarely go for the target directly. They compromise whatever is easiest (a helpdesk
account, an old service account, a workstation) and follow permissions from there. A single
leftover delegation, for example a support group with local admin rights on a sensitive server,
turns any compromise of that support group into a compromise of the zone (Account Manipulation,
MITRE ATT&CK T1098, and Remote Services, T1021). The finding's exposure figure shows how many
users and computers can reach the controlling account or group.

## Before you change anything
- Decide whether the account or group **should** administer the zone. If it should, add it to the zone
  on the Privilege Zones page (and protect it like the zone) instead of removing the permission.
- Find what uses the right: for local admin rights, check 4624 logon events (type 3/10) on the
  computer. For AD permissions, check the account's role with its owner.
- If the finding says the permission is **inherited**, change it on the parent OU or container.
  That change affects every object below it, so review those objects too.

## Fix
### Option A: Remove the right (recommended)
The finding's "fix" line names the relationship. Common cases:
1. **AD permission (ACE):** In **Active Directory Users and Computers**, enable **View > Advanced
   Features**, open the object (or the parent OU for inherited ACEs) > **Security** > **Advanced**,
   select the entry for the account or group and click **Remove**.
2. **Local Administrators / Remote Desktop Users:** remove the account or group from the local group
   on the computer, or from the GPO / Intune policy that adds it (**Computer Configuration >
   Preferences > Control Panel Settings > Local Users and Groups**, or **Restricted Groups**).
3. **Group membership (AddMember on a zone group):** remove the permission from the group's
   Security tab as in step 1.
4. **GPO link / GPO edit rights:** restrict who can edit the linked GPO
   (**Group Policy Management > GPO > Delegation**), or unlink it from the zone OU.

### Option B: Bring the account or group into the zone
If the account or group legitimately manages the zone, add it to the zone on the Privilege Zones
page. Then manage it with the same controls as the zone: no use from lower-tier computers, and admin accounts dedicated to this tier.

### PowerShell
```powershell
Import-Module ActiveDirectory

# Show ACEs for the account or group on a zone object
$dn = (Get-ADObject -Filter "Name -eq '<Object>'").DistinguishedName
(Get-Acl "AD:$dn").Access | Where-Object { $_.IdentityReference -like "*<Account>*" } |
    Select-Object IdentityReference, ActiveDirectoryRights, IsInherited

# Remove those ACEs (explicit ones only; fix inherited ones on the parent)
$acl = Get-Acl "AD:$dn"
$acl.Access | Where-Object { $_.IdentityReference -like "*<Account>*" -and -not $_.IsInherited } |
    ForEach-Object { [void]$acl.RemoveAccessRule($_) }
Set-Acl -Path "AD:$dn" -AclObject $acl

# Local admin rights on a zone computer (run on that computer)
Remove-LocalGroupMember -Group "Administrators" -Member "<Domain>\<Account>"
```

## Verify
Re-run the `Get-Acl` command above, or `Get-LocalGroupMember -Group "Administrators"` on the
computer, and confirm the account or group is gone. Collect again (or use Retest on the finding) and confirm the finding ID is gone.
The zone's row in the report shows the accounts that still have an attack path to the zone.

## Rollback
Before removing an ACE, export it: `(Get-Acl "AD:$dn").Sddl | Out-File acl-backup.txt`. Restore
it with `$acl.SetSecurityDescriptorSddlForm((Get-Content acl-backup.txt))` followed by `Set-Acl`.
Re-add local group members with `Add-LocalGroupMember`.
